# Framework and evidence safeguards

Use this reference before naming a framework, scale, method, endorsement, or causal claim. Verify current primary sources.

## Claim discipline

| Claim type | Minimum discipline |
|---|---|
| Reach or delivery | Complete, deduplicated records and a defined unit of count |
| Outcome change | Same construct at baseline and follow-up; report missing data/attrition |
| Contribution | Theory of change, rival explanations, context, and evidence of the programme’s role |
| Causal impact | Appropriate counterfactual/comparison plus sufficiently rigorous analysis |
| Cost saving or SROI | Evidenced outcomes, transparent proxies, counterfactual adjustments, sensitivity analysis |
| 預防 / 延後問題 | Baseline risk, timing evidence, and a rival-explanation check; do not equate “no incident during the project” with prevention |
| 內化 / 遷移 | Use beyond the original task or prompt; not workshop satisfaction |
| 賦權 / 去中心化 | Change in decision rights, voice, or dependency — not attendance at a participatory event |
| 增值 / 可見化 | Evidence of worth or being seen by a named audience; not a single communications output |

Do not promote 量的變化 to full impact, or outcome change to causal impact or financial value, without the additional evidence required.

## Eight-dimension coding vs other frameworks

The eight change dimensions are the primary classification. Other frameworks are optional lenses:

- **Theory of change / logic model:** Explain why activities should produce selected cells in the 影響方向 × 對象 matrix and make assumptions testable.
- **IMP Five Dimensions (What, Who, How Much, Contribution, Risk):** Use to stress-test materiality, underserved groups, scale/depth/duration, plausible contribution, and downside. In short grant fields, write this in plain language.
- **SROI:** Use only when outcomes are evidenced and financial proxies are defensible. Include deadweight, attribution, displacement and drop-off where relevant, and sensitivity analysis. Never present an anticipated ratio as a guarantee.
- **Sector or funder frameworks (e.g. NCSS, SDG, ESG):** Apply only when they match the service context. Confirm current outcome/tool recommendations from the primary source. Do not call a measure “recommended” merely because it is relevant.
- **Client or academic frameworks:** Use as a secondary structure. If they conflict with stakeholder priorities or with the eight dimensions, state the limitation and keep measurement meaningful and ethical.

Do not list framework acronyms in a constrained application field unless the funder asked for them.

## Sensitive services and populations

Use enhanced safeguards for health, mental health, disability, child/youth services, violence prevention, homelessness, migration, justice involvement, financial hardship, or other high-risk contexts.

- Match data-collection burden to service intensity and staff capacity to respond.
- Specify consent, confidentiality, access control, retention, incident response, safeguarding, and referral routes.
- Do not make a diagnostic claim from a non-diagnostic questionnaire.
- Treat pre/post change as indicative unless sample, follow-up, comparison, and analysis support stronger inference.
- Check accessibility, language, cultural fit, licence, and stigma before selecting tools.

## Equity, power, and environmental effects

- Involve affected objects when defining material outcomes, especially for 關係性變化 (權力、賦權、依賴) and 價值與意義變化.
- Assess reach, benefit, burden, and harm across material subgroups; disaggregate only where consent, privacy, sample size, and interpretation permit.
- Include environmental outcomes when the intervention plausibly affects resource use, emissions, ecosystems, or environmental justice — often as 活化／再生 or as 質/量 of environmental condition. Do not add generic green claims without a pathway and indicator.

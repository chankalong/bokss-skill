# IMP 五大維度, SROI, and NCSS SEF

Read this when the user, funder, or context names IMP, 五大維度, SROI, Social Value, NCSS, SEF, or asks for standardised SSA outcome tools. Verify current primary sources before claiming a metric is recommended, validated, or endorsed.

Primary sources:

- IMP / Impact Frontiers: [Five dimensions of impact](https://impactfrontiers.org/norms/five-dimensions-of-impact/)
- SROI / Social Value International: [Principles of Social Value](https://www.socialvalueint.org/principles) and the Guide to SROI
- NCSS SEF: [Sector Evaluation Framework](https://www.ncss.gov.sg/research-and-insights/research-and-evaluation/sector-evaluation-framework/) (page last updated 29 May 2026 at time of writing)

## How the lenses stack

| Lens | Job | Do not use it to |
|---|---|---|
| Eight change dimensions | Classify **what kind** of change occurred, for which 對象 | Prove attribution or financial return |
| IMP 五大維度 | Stress-test materiality: What, Who, How Much, Contribution, Risk | Replace the eight-dimension map |
| NCSS SEF | Pick a **standardised sector metric** when the service matches a Singapore SSA programme group | Force ZBI/ACT SG/C-SSRS onto unmatched programmes or other countries |
| SROI | Value evidenced outcomes in financial terms after a credible outcome model exists | Promise a ratio, or value outcomes that are not evidenced |

In a constrained grant field, write the substance in plain language. Name the acronym only if the funder asked for it.

## IMP 五大維度

Impact is a change in an outcome caused by an organisation. It can be positive or negative, intended or unintended. Use the five dimensions on every material cell of the 影響方向 × 對象 matrix.

| Dimension | English | Ask | Typical data |
|---|---|---|---|
| 什麼 | What | Which outcome? Positive or negative? How important is it to the people (or planet) experiencing it? | Outcome statement; stakeholder importance; threshold / sustainable goal |
| 誰 | Who | Who experiences it? How underserved are they relative to the outcome? Do results differ by group? | Stakeholder type; geography; baseline well-being; equity characteristics |
| 多少 | How Much | Scale (how many), depth (degree of change), duration (how long) | Reach; magnitude; retention / follow-up length |
| 貢獻 | Contribution | Was the outcome likely better than what would have occurred otherwise? | Counterfactual, comparison, or contribution analysis. Do not confuse with 多少 depth (baseline vs later, ignoring other factors) |
| 風險 | Risk | Likelihood that impact will differ from expectation, in a way that is material to people or planet | Likelihood × severity (Low / Medium / High) per outcome |

How Much splits into **規模 (scale)**、**深度 (depth)**、**持續 (duration)**. Map them onto 量的變化 and 時間性變化 without collapsing the eight-dimension codes.

Contribution overlaps SROI **deadweight / attribution / additionality**. Say which method was used (comparison group, benchmark, theory-based contribution analysis). Depth of change ≠ contribution.

### Impact risk

Assess likelihood and severity for each expected outcome. Start with **evidence risk** (not enough high-quality data across What / Who / How Much / Contribution). If evidence risk is High, other data-dependent risks are likely High too.

Impact Frontiers names ten risk types, including: evidence; community participation; external; drop-off; efficiency; execution; alignment; endurance; unexpected impact; inequity. Name only the risks that are material. Community participation, external, endurance, and alignment can be judged from the model and operating environment even when outcome data are thin.

In short applications, cover IMP in one operational sentence: who is underserved, what change, how much, what would likely happen anyway, and one material risk.

## SROI

Use SROI only after a credible outcome model and evidence plan exist. Treat the ratio as an evidence-backed estimate, not a promised return.

### Principles of Social Value (SVI)

1. Involve stakeholders
2. Understand what changes
3. Value the things that matter
4. Only include what is material
5. Do not overclaim
6. Be transparent
7. Verify the result
8. Be responsive — use the account to decide, not only to report

Principles 1–5 account for value; 6–7 report it; 8 manages it.

### Process (Guide to SROI)

1. Scope and stakeholders
2. Map outcomes (impact map: inputs → outputs → outcomes)
3. Evidence outcomes and give them a value (indicators, duration, financial proxies)
4. Establish impact (adjustments below)
5. Calculate SROI (project, discount, ratio, sensitivity, optional payback)
6. Report, use, embed; seek assurance where claimed

### Impact adjustments (do not overclaim)

| Adjustment | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Deadweight | Share that would have happened anyway |
| Attribution | Share caused by other organisations or people |
| Displacement | Benefits here that reduce benefits elsewhere |
| Drop-off | Outcome fades in later years |
| Leakage | Benefits accruing outside the intended group (include when material) |

Document proxy sources. Run sensitivity on the largest assumptions. Do not present an anticipated ratio as a guarantee. Do not monetise 心理與態度 or 價值與意義 cells with a generic proxy when stakeholders have not confirmed that the outcome and the proxy match.

## NCSS Sector Evaluation Framework (SEF)

Singapore National Council of Social Service framework. Common language for SSAs, funders, and government to measure sector outcomes, track service-user progress, and articulate impact.

Two components:

1. **Sector outcomes** — standardised, statistically assessed metrics for programmes with similar objectives. For similar theories of change, SEF proposes a single outcome measure.
2. **Monitoring indicator** — sector-wide quality of life: **WHOQOL-BREF** (adults 18+) and **KIDSCREEN-27** (under 18), comparable to NCSS national benchmarks.

Apply SEF only when the service domain matches. Do not call a tool “NCSS recommended” merely because it is validated or locally known. Confirm the current programme-group table on the NCSS page before naming a metric.

### Sector outcome metrics (NCSS page, 29 May 2026)

| Programme group | Outcome / objective | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| Caregiver support | Improve management of caregiver burden | Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI) |
| Children & youth | Improve positive youth development competencies | ACT SG |
| Children in care | Reduce child’s needs | Child and Adolescent Needs and Strengths (CANS) |
| Early intervention | Improve child development outcomes | Assessment scores or achievement of milestones |
| Employment support | Obtain employment and stay employed | Sustained employment for 3 or 6 consecutive months |
| Family resilience | Improve family resilience | Walsh Family Resilience Questionnaire (WFRQ) |
| Family support | Improve families’ social and emotional needs | Family and Adult Support Tool (FAST) |
| Family violence | Reduce the occurrence of violence | Occurrence of violence within 12 months of case closure |
| Marriage support | Ensure couples remain married | Couple remain intact within 3 years of attending the programme |
| Mental health support | Reduce psychological distress | Feedback Informed Treatment – Outcome Rating Scale (FIT-ORS) |
| Recidivism | Reduce the risk of recidivism | YLS/CMI or LS/CMI |
| School absenteeism | Improve school attendance | Sustained school attendance for 3 or 6 consecutive months |
| Suicide crisis support | Reduce suicide risk | Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) |

Tote Board’s Impact Measurement Framework is aligned with these SEF metrics for some grant routes. Alignment is not automatic permission to use a licensed clinical tool.

### SEF safeguards

- Match **programme group**, not a nearby theme. A school workshop is not automatically Children & youth / ACT SG.
- Psychometric tools need licence, training, language version, and a plan to act on scores. C-SSRS, CANS, YLS/CMI, LS/CMI, and similar instruments require enhanced safeguarding and must not be used as casual grant decoration.
- Pre/post change on a SEF metric is indicative unless design, sample, follow-up, and analysis support stronger inference.
- WHOQOL-BREF / KIDSCREEN-27 monitor QOL; they are not a substitute for the programme-group outcome metric.
- If the intervention is education, design, 地方創生, or research contribution outside SSA programme groups, keep the eight-dimension map as primary and do not import SEF tools.

## When the user names all three

1. Fill 影響方向 × 對象 first.
2. Run IMP 五大維度 on each material cell (especially Who, Contribution, Risk).
3. If the service is a matching Singapore SSA programme, select the SEF metric and QOL monitor; map that metric onto the matching eight-dimension cell (often 心理與態度, 能力與行為, or 量／時間).
4. Add SROI only if outcomes are evidenced, proxies are defensible, and the decision actually needs valuation.
